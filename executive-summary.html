01 · Peer Benchmarking Methodology

How we established the peer benchmark —
a collaborative, segment-matched approach.

Developed collaboratively with the Dixstone team across two phases — Phase 1 internal diagnostic (SAP-direct, per rig; reviewed with the Dixstone General Manager and drilling team), Phase 2 external peer benchmark (9 listed contractors mapped to Dixstone's segment mix). The methodology is segment-matched and multi-dimensional: premium jackups vs premium jackups, standard jackups vs standard jackups, land rigs vs land rigs — combining audited financials, operational performance, workforce density, and capital intensity into one apples-to-apples view. Not generic industry averages.

Collaborative · two phases

Built alongside the Dixstone team. Phase 1 — internal diagnostic, bottom-up from Dixstone's SAP exports (MB52 inventory, MB51 movements, ME2N purchase orders), per rig. Phase 2 — external peer benchmark, 9 listed contractors. Both phases reviewed with Dixstone leadership ahead of this synthesis.

Phase 1 + Phase 2 · GM-reviewed

Apples-to-apples · segment matched

Each rig benchmarked against its direct peer set, not the industry at large. Premium JU (AXIMA) vs Borr · ADNOC · ADES · Valaris-JU · Velesto. Standard JU (BANBA, NUADA) vs Shelf · Valaris · ADES. Land (MIDIR, DRAVUS) vs Helmerich & Payne · Nabors · Precision. Platform (LUG) vs comparable HP/Nabors operations.

4 segments · direct peer sets

Multi-dimensional · 18 KPIs

Combines audited financial filings (10-K, 10-Q, 20-F from 11 regulators), operational performance (NPT, utilisation, dayrate), workforce density (FTE per rig), and capital intensity (CAPEX / revenue) into a single comparable view. DFA-1 — Sigma's agentic AI Drilling Financial Analyst — fills inference gaps where direct disclosure is absent, validated against Sigma's hands-on drilling experience.

04 · Four Opportunities to FY26

Building on the trajectory and wins —
$15–22M of Year-1 opportunity value.

Each opportunity is segment-matched, quantified, age-aware where it matters (the jackup fleet is materially older than peer fleets — BANBA 42y, NUADA 47y — while land rigs are younger than US-land peers), and tied to a specific operational lever. Two improve the balance sheet; two structurally improve the P&L. Combined Year-1 value: $5–10M of one-time release and reclassification plus $9–12M of recurring annual P&L improvement — a credible path to peer-median margin over 24–36 months.

01 · Working Capital · Forward PO Control + Categorised Dead Stock

Control the forward flow; categorise existing stock before releasing it.

West Africa logistics make wholesale disposal unrealistic — a single Class-A critical spare prevents weeks of $100K/day NPT, a 100× insurance ratio against the carry cost. The structural lever is forward: consumption-driven PO discipline at order (past-consumption check at 6M/12M/36M, cross-plant SAP visibility, country-level min/max) avoiding $2–4M/yr of new over-stocking. For the existing $22.5M pile, a 90-day A/B/C/D criticality categorisation by OEM status, regional sourceability, and cross-rig fungibility leads to a measured $2–4M selective release (D-class via regional operator resale and OEM buyback). Capital items currently miscoded on the inventory line ($3–6M — engines, BOP-grade components) reclass to the asset register.

02 · Land · Right-Sizing on Young Assets

Right-size MIDIR & DRAVUS crewing toward peer benchmark.

MIDIR 72 FTE on a 15-year-old rig; DRAVUS 66 FTE on an 11-year-old rig. US-land peers (HP / Nabors / Precision) run 50–55 FTE on modern fleets of comparable vintage. Fleet age is not the explanation — the gap is structural. Combination of expat-ratio review, shore-overhead reallocation, and crew-rotation model — recovers ~$3M/yr to fleet RDM.

03 · NPT Recovery · Vintage Jackups

Close BANBA & NUADA NPT to an age-realistic target.

BANBA (42y) at 8.7% NPT and NUADA (47y) at 5.8% — peer median 3.0% is measured on much younger jackup fleets, so an age-realistic target is 4–5%, not peer-best. Sustaining-CAPEX lift on Cat IV recerts, CMMS rollout (MM Fusion), OEM-anchored PM. Each percentage point of NPT recovered ≈ $1M/yr at $100K dayrate.

04 · CAPEX · Reallocate Toward Aged Jackups

Lift jackup sustaining CAPEX; scrutinise land spend on young assets.

Premium & standard JU under-investing (4–6% vs 7–9% norm) — acutely concerning given BANBA (42y) and NUADA (47y) need sustaining capital to remain operational. Conversely, MIDIR (15y) and DRAVUS (11y) running CAPEX at 8–22% on mid-life land assets is harder to justify on age grounds. A six-week line-item diagnostic on land spend, then redirect ~$1.5M/yr from land toward jackup sustaining where the assets are aging.

Strategic flag · FY27+ board discussion BANBA (built 1984 · 42y) and NUADA (built 1979 · 47y) sit at or beyond typical economic life for jackups. Sustaining-CAPEX and SPS (Special Periodical Survey) costs compound non-linearly past age 35. Fleet-succession planning — whether to continue extending these hulls, acquire second-hand premium replacements, or commission newbuilds — sits outside this Year-1 P&L analysis but is the strategic decision facing the board in the FY27+ window. The Year-1 opportunities above are designed to maximise economic value from the current fleet while that decision is taken.
